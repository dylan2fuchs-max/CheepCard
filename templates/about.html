💡 The Founder's Story

Hi there! I want to first and foremost want to say thank you for checking out CheepCards. This project has taken hours, days, and months to complete, and I just want to say the biggest thank you for checking out this site.

To start off, my name is Dylan—or I will also go by Silver Knight within the news log. I have a major background in recreation, and over the past year, I have merged my understanding of recreation with computer programming.

CheepCards is a site created for the interest and usage of users like yourself, providing the ability to search Magic: The Gathering cards in seconds and find the options that will fulfill your brewing inspirations. It allows you to break down what a deck can do for functionality with the synergy function, and lets you search for the most budget-friendly or priciest cards.

This is all done with the intent to accomplish three goals. First, I wanted to provide a resource for the community to use—a resource for brewers and thinkers to find cards that won't empty their pockets but will create fun experiences. That is why there is a free tier to use on this site.

Ranking System

For those joining the CheepCards site, you will be introduced to a ranking system created by myself. This system establishes fast keywords and variables to help with building and defining many of the currently 30,000+ cards within Magic: The Gathering.

I want to first preface by saying this ranking was created at face value, trying to keep personal objectivity out of the equation as much as possible. It is completely understandable if you may not agree with every card's ranking. In addition, even though these rankings share some similarities with the 1–5 bracket system, they are in no way, shape, or form supposed to reflect those categories. Instead, these rankings are placed on cards to help understand their ceiling and floor—how they operate—rather than labeling whether they are the best, a Bracket 5, a Bracket 4, and so on.

Does this mean some powerful cards will fall somewhat into those high-power categories? Correct. But they are not meant to be officially listed as such.

Before I dive into the reasoning for each class and their rankings, I want to give an example. One card that I think gives a great understanding of this system is Aesi, Tyrant of Gyre Strait. Aesi is widely considered a very strong and powerful card. With that said, it falls under the Draw class due to its ability to draw cards when lands are played. Aesi is a card with a very high ceiling, but also the power to come online in seconds. Thus, why it received an average ranking. Even considering that Aesi is a force to be reckoned with and could receive a higher ranking, I wanted to evaluate it at face value—accounting for its high mana cost and its ability to impact the game the moment it hits the battlefield.

As you go through the rankings, you will see many cards that could be considered higher or lower, but many ranks may turn out differently due to the floor-to-ceiling nature of how they function. I will note that in the future, I am considering adding other classes, as the current ones don't cover every card's nature. Some already on my radar include cantrip spells and burst draw (cards that draw a large amount of cards in one turn) versus steady draw (cards that consistently draw cards, but maybe only once a turn).

Here is the ranking breakdown for each class:

Lands

  • Optimized: These lands are the most flexible and reliable. Mostly gathering cards like Command Tower, original dual lands, and fetch lands.
  • Fast: Very functional and fast lands, but may come with additional costs or conditions for their speed.
  • Average: Lands that a majority (around 80% of the time) will enter the battlefield untapped and serve as a great resource.
  • Passive: These lands sit on the dividing line between running them or just running basics. They are often just as good as a basic—or you might as well just consider running a basic.
  • Low: As shared, these are very low on the rankings, but that does not mean they still can't be used.

Ramp

  • Optimized: Cost-efficient and requires very few resources to get functioning (1–2 mana).
  • Fast: Can be used very early on (1–2 mana) and allows for additional resources right away.
  • Average: Cards that provide fast mana, but may take a turn to use or operate. Big, splashy amounts of mana also fit this pool.
  • Passive: Requires several turns to get online, but is still functional.
  • Low: A large cost difference compared to the resulting mana you obtain relative to other cards.

Draw

  • Optimized: Does not need help to operate and can grant you multiple cards with little effort.
  • Fast: A big, explosive number of cards spanning 1–3 mana.
  • Average: A solid burst of draw all at once, or consistent draw every turn.
  • Passive: Requires a piece of setup before drawing.
  • Low: Little burst, or requires a lot of setup for draws.

Removal / Board Wipes

  • Optimized: Cost-effective and gets the job done or hits multiple targets.
  • Fast: Fast interaction ranging between 1–3 mana for single-target removal.
  • Average: Reliable, but may require holding up mana.
  • Passive: Not as cost-effective, but handles getting rid of a permanent well.
  • Low: Slower and less efficient removal.
